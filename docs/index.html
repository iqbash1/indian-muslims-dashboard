The state of Muslim India, in data

India's roughly 20 crore (200 million) Muslims, or 14.2% of the population (Census 2011), trail the all-India average on 12 of the 16 indicators that allow a direct comparison, widest on Lok Sabha Muslim share, higher-education enrolment rate, and state MLA Muslim share. They run ahead on a handful (toilet access, infant mortality rate, and anaemia in women).

Each card sets the latest Muslim figure beside Hindu and all-India baselines, drawn from 16 primary government sources. Open any card for its chart, method and source. How these are measured →

About this measurement

Definition. Percentage of each community living in urban areas. Census Table C-01 provides religion × residence (rural/urban) × sex breakdowns at national, state, district, and town levels; series covers Census 2001 and 2011.

Methodology. Urban share = urban_persons / total_persons * 100, per Census C-01. Two-census series (2001 and 2011) at national and state level; the 2021 Census is delayed. Muslims (~40%) and Christians (~40%) are markedly more urbanised than the all-India average (~31%), a Sachar-era fact that conditions outcomes downstream (urban-poor labour markets, segregation, housing access).

About this measurement

Definition. Percentage of live births in the years preceding each survey round that took place in a health facility (public or private), among women age 15-49.

Methodology. Time series across four NFHS rounds: NFHS-2 (1998-99), NFHS-3 (2005-06), NFHS-4 (2015-16) and NFHS-5 (2019-21); years 1998/2005/2015/2020 are the round labels. About this measurement

Definition. Gross Enrolment Ratio = student enrolment in higher education / population in the 18-23 age cohort * 100. The AISHE-published headline GER. Higher education means any course after Class 12: degree, diploma, and certificate programmes at universities, colleges, and standalone institutions.

Methodology. Numerator: AISHE 2021-22 published All-India "Muslim Minority" enrolment (Muslim 21,08,033; total 4,32,68,181 per Report p29 prose). Denominator: Census 2011 C-15 (15-19 + 20-24) × 0.6 as an 18-23 effective-cohort proxy; single-year-age table (C-13) not pulled and 2021 Census deferred. The older 2011 denominator makes our computed GER run a few points ABOVE the officially published figures (national 27-28%; published Muslim Minority GER around 9% in recent AISHE rounds). The absolute level is a computation, not an official figure, but the cross-religion gap (Muslim enrolment runs at roughly one-third the national rate) is robust to the choice of denominator and is the dashboard story. The card also sets Muslims beside "Other minorities" (Christian, Sikh, Buddhist and Jain, 9,05,159 students), which AISHE reports only as one grouped category and never splits: Muslims enrol at roughly three-quarters their rate. AISHE publishes no separate Hindu figure, so there is no Hindu bar.

About this measurement

Definition. Total number of students classified as "Muslim Minority" enrolled in higher education institutions, per AISHE annual reporting.

Methodology. AISHE classifies enrolment as "Muslim Minority" vs "Other Minority Community" (Christians, Sikhs, Buddhists, Jains, Parsis), with Hindu enrolment implied as residual. This metric publishes only the absolute Muslim enrolment. Muslim *share* of total enrolment is a separate metric that requires Total Enrolment by state (separate AISHE table not yet extracted).

About this measurement

Definition. Average monthly spending per person (Monthly Per Capita Consumption Expenditure, MPCE). In India this is the standard measure of economic well-being, used in place of income, for which no reliable figure by religion exists.

Methodology. Sachar Committee (2006), Chapter 8, from the NSS 61st round (2004-05): all-India average MPCE by socio-religious category. Muslims Rs.635 vs the all-India average Rs.712, near SC/ST (Rs.520) and far below upper-caste 'Hindu General' households (Rs.1023); the gap is sharpest in urban areas. This is the most recent figure published by religion: later rounds (HCES 2022-23) break spending down by social group (SC/ST/OBC/Others) only, not by religion, so a current religion-wise figure needs the unit-level microdata. Rupees are 2004-05 nominal (not inflation-adjusted).
