The state of Muslim India, in data

India's roughly 200 million Muslims, or 14.2% of the population (Census 2011), trail the all-India average on 12 of the 16 indicators that allow a direct comparison, widest on Lok Sabha Muslim share, higher-education enrolment rate, and state MLA Muslim share. They run ahead on a handful (toilet access, infant mortality rate, and anaemia in women).

Each card sets the latest Muslim figure beside Hindu and all-India baselines, drawn from 17 primary government sources. Open any card for its chart, method and source. How these are measured →

About this measurement

Definition. Percentage of each community living in urban areas. Census 2011 Table C-01 provides religion × residence (rural/urban) × sex breakdowns at national, state, district, and town levels.

Methodology. Urban share = urban_persons / total_persons * 100, per Census 2011 C-01. Single-year metric (no time series, 2021 Census delayed). Muslims (~40%) and Christians (~40%) are markedly more urbanised than the all-India average (~31%), a Sachar-era fact that conditions outcomes downstream (urban-poor labour markets, segregation, housing access).

India had 640 districts in 2011, yet the 100 most Muslim-populous, just 16% of them, are home to 58.5% of all Indian Muslims. This is a geographic-concentration measure, not a community comparison.

About this measurement

Definition. Share of national Muslim population that lives in the top 100 districts ranked by absolute Muslim population. High values = geographically concentrated; low values = dispersed.

Methodology. Computed by sorting all ~640 Indian districts by absolute Muslim population (per Census 2011 state MDDS C-1 files), taking the top 100, summing their Muslim residents, and dividing by national Muslim total. Denominator field on canonical row records exact numerator (top-100 sum) and denominator (national Muslim count).

About this measurement

Definition. Gross Enrolment Ratio = student enrolment in higher education / population in the 18-23 age cohort * 100. The AISHE-published headline GER.

Methodology. Numerator: AISHE 2021-22 published All-India "Muslim Minority" enrolment (Muslim 21,08,033; total 4,32,68,181 per Report p29 prose). Denominator: Census 2011 C-15 (15-19 + 20-24) × 0.6 as an 18-23 effective-cohort proxy; single-year-age table (C-13) not pulled and 2021 Census deferred. The older 2011 denominator makes our computed GER run a few points ABOVE the officially published figures (national 27-28%; published Muslim Minority GER around 9% in recent AISHE rounds). The absolute level is a computation, not an official figure, but the cross-religion gap (Muslim enrolment runs at roughly one-third the national rate) is robust to the choice of denominator and is the dashboard story.

How much a typical person in a community spends in a month, on food, rent, fuel, clothes and everything else. In India this is the usual way to measure how well-off people are, because reliable income data does not exist.

₹635spent per person each month(2004)

About this measurement

Definition. Average monthly spending per person (Monthly Per Capita Consumption Expenditure, MPCE). In India this is the standard measure of economic well-being, used in place of income, for which no reliable figure by religion exists.

Methodology. Sachar Committee (2006), Chapter 8, from the NSS 61st round (2004-05): all-India average MPCE by socio-religious category. Muslims Rs.635 vs the all-India average Rs.712, near SC/ST (Rs.520) and far below upper-caste 'Hindu General' households (Rs.1023); the gap is sharpest in urban areas. This is the most recent figure published by religion: later rounds (HCES 2022-23) break spending down by social group (SC/ST/OBC/Others) only, not by religion, so a current religion-wise figure needs the unit-level microdata. Rupees are 2004-05 nominal (not inflation-adjusted).

Methodology. Manual-entry metric, like ls-share: religion is not officially tabulated by ECI or PRS. Underlying data is candidate-affidavit religion classified post-election by journalists (Maktoob, Clarion, India Forum, The Wire, Deccan Herald, Outlook, Radiance Weekly, Free Press Journal, ummid.com, thenewzradar). National aggregate ~6% across the 30 covered assemblies. Highest: J&K 60% (2024, first election since Article 370 reorg); Kerala 25%, Assam 24.6%. Lowest: HP / Goa / Mizoram / Nagaland / Sikkim / Arunachal / Chhattisgarh / Tripura, all 0% (most have never elected a Muslim MLA in state history). Each row's methodology_note cites the source compilation(s) used.

About this measurement

Definition. Incarceration rate: prisoners of religion R per 100,000 of religion R's total population. More direct measure of disproportion than "share of prisoners", comparable across religions without needing to know population baselines.

Methodology. Cross-source: NCRB PSI 2022 total prisoner count (convicts + undertrials + detenues + other prisoners; STATES + UTs subtotals = ALL-INDIA by construction) divided by Census 2011 national religious population times 100,000. The 'denominator' column on each canonical row records the exact (count, population) pair used. Caveat: Maharashtra non-reporting of religion for ~33k undertrials/detenues, so the religion-reported numerator excludes those; full national population is the denominator.

About this measurement

Definition. Number of communal/religious rioting incidents recorded by NCRB under IPC Sec.147-151 (rioting), specifically the "Communal/Religious" sub-classification. National annual totals (2020-2022) plus state-level breakdown for 2022.

Methodology. NCRB Crime in India 2022, Table 1.2 (national, 2020-2022). Incident counts only, with no religion of victim/perpetrator in the published table. Caveat: several states stopped recording 'communal' as a separate crime category since ~2017, which deflates the national total over time. Civil-society compilations (Documentation of the Oppressed, India Hate Lab) typically report higher counts.

About this measurement

Definition. Annual count of verified in-person anti-Muslim hate speech events documented by India Hate Lab (IHL). Includes political rallies, religious gatherings, and electoral events with hateful rhetoric.
