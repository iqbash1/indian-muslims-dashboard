About this measurement

Definition. What an average household owns (land, buildings, livestock, machinery, vehicles, financial assets) minus what it owes, as on mid-2012 (the survey ran through 2013). Wealth is the stock that spending cannot show, and wealth gaps compound across generations, so they run far wider than spending gaps.

Methodology. Computed from the unit-level microdata of AIDIS 2013 (All-India Debt & Investment Survey, NSS 70th round, Visit 1; 1.11 lakh households) via the MoSPI NADA API: average household assets minus outstanding debt, as on 30.06.2012 (so year=2012; the survey was fielded in 2013). The published report breaks wealth down by social group but never by religion. The estimator reproduces the published all-India average debt and incidence of indebtedness exactly, and average assets within 0.75% (a documented empty-column defect in MoSPI's own CSV conversion of the non-farm-equipment block, worth exactly the missing block's published share). Muslim households held INR 9.16 lakh against the all-India INR 13.95 lakh (66%) and Hindu INR 13.69 lakh (67%); the gap is overwhelmingly urban - urban Muslim households hold 51% of urban Hindu net worth (INR 11.5 vs 22.5 lakh) while rural is 83%. Gold and ornaments sit outside the published asset concept (about 4% on top); land is valued at guideline rather than market rates. Values rounded to the nearest INR 1,000. NSO unit-data rider: religion is self-reported and the survey is stratified for states, so the split is indicative with no sub-state estimates. AIDIS 2018-19 would extend this to a trend, but its NADA distribution is a proprietary binary; its published all-India anchors are recorded in the repo for that future build. The "Borrowing sources" tab folds in the companion measure from the same AIDIS 2013 microdata (the decarded institutional-credit-share metric): the institutional share of outstanding cash debt among indebted households, reproducing the published all-India shares exactly (rural 56.0 / urban 84.5). Muslim 68.3% vs Hindu 72.4%, with the gap urban (72.7 vs 85.4); Muslims also borrow the least overall (23.8% of households indebted vs Hindu 29.0%). Credit-agency code 09 "others" is non-institutional despite its position in the code list (the layout trap is documented in the repo).

Sources. AIDIS 2013 (NSS 70th). Data file: household-net-worth.csv.
