About this measurement

Definition. Of the money indebted households owe, the share borrowed from institutional lenders (banks, co-operatives, government and related agencies) rather than moneylenders, shopkeepers, relatives or other informal sources. Informal credit is costlier and unprotected, so a lower share signals weaker access to the formal financial system, a gap the Sachar Committee flagged for Muslims.

Methodology. Computed from AIDIS 2013 (NSS 70th round, Visit 1) unit-level microdata via the MoSPI NADA API: institutional share of outstanding cash debt as on 30.06.2012 (year=2012), among indebted households. Reproduces the published all-India shares exactly (rural 56.0 / urban 84.5, KI(70/18.2) Table 8); the published tables stop at state and social group, never religion. Muslim 68.3% vs Hindu 72.4%; the urban contrast is the story - 72.7% of urban Muslim debt is institutional vs 85.4% for urban Hindus, meaning urban Muslims lean far more on informal lenders. Context: Muslims also borrow least overall (23.8% of households indebted vs Hindu 29.0%). Credit-agency code 09 "others" is non-institutional despite its position in the code list (the layout trap is documented in the repo). Religion x residence cells ride on indebted households only (about 1,700 rural Muslim), so read sub-cuts as indicative. NSO unit-data rider applies: religion is self-reported; no sub-state estimates.

Sources. AIDIS 2013 (NSS 70th). Data file: institutional-credit-share.csv.
